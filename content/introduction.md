# A Cookbook Built Like Software

This isn't just a collection of recipes; it's a system for your kitchen. As a software engineer who loves to cook, I've always been fascinated by the patterns that underpin great meals. The best dishes are rarely built from scratch every single time. Instead, they rely on a handful of foundational recipes, or "staples," that can be prepared in advance and used in a variety of ways.

This cookbook is built on that very principle.

## The Staples Philosophy

At the heart of this book is the concept of "staples": foundational recipes like rich chicken stock, a delicious and flavourful herb butter (ideally with herbs from the garden), or a versatile mint-garlic yoghurt. These are the building blocks, the reusable components, that will empower you to create a wide range of dishes with less effort and more confidence.

This approach is intentional, and it's designed to help you in a few key ways:

1.  **It Reduces Cognitive Load.** By mastering a few key staples, you'll find that many of the dishes in this book become simpler and faster to prepare. You're not starting from zero every time you cook.
2.  **It Encourages Mastery.** These staples are the foundation of countless classic and modern dishes. By focusing on them, you're not just learning recipes; you're learning the fundamental techniques that will make you a better and more intuitive cook.
3.  **It Powers Meal Planning.** The "Snap to Plan" web experience is designed to work with this philosophy. When you scan a recipe's Snap Code, you can instantly build menus and shopping lists that leverage the staples you may have already prepared, streamlining your weekly cooking.
4.  **It Embodies "Software You Can Eat."** This system of components (`Staples`), builds (`Dishes`), and compositions (`Menus`) is the same mental model that software engineers use to build complex and reliable applications. We're applying those same principles to the art of cooking.

## How to Use This Book

You don't have to start by making every single staple. The recipes in this book stand on their own. But if you embrace the system, you'll find a new rhythm in your kitchen.

Try making one or two staples at the start of the week. See how they can be used to create a quick lunch, elevate a simple dinner, or form the base of a more elaborate weekend meal.

This is more than a cookbook. It's a new way to think about your cooking. It's about building a library of flavours and techniques that you can draw on every day.

It's software you can eat.
