## Raw Idea
Kent pumpkin wedges tossed in olive oil and warm spice mix, roasted until caramelized and tender. Served on a platter over mint garlic yoghurt, scattered with pomegranate seeds, toasted pepitas, fresh herbs. Impressive centrepiece that's actually simple — two staples do all the work. Great with flatbread for scooping.

## Overview
The impressive vegetarian showstopper that looks like you spent hours but takes 15 minutes of actual work. Spiced pumpkin wedges roast while you make yoghurt sauce and prep garnishes. The assembly is where it shines — a colorful, textured platter that works as main or spectacular side. The warm spice mix and mint yoghurt are the flavor foundations.

## Why This Works
High heat caramelizes the pumpkin's natural sugars while the warm spices toast and deepen. Keeping skin on gives structure and creates crispy edges. The cool, tangy mint yoghurt contrasts with sweet, spiced pumpkin. Pomegranate adds tart bursts. Pepitas add crunch. Fresh herbs add brightness. It's a study in contrasts — hot/cold, sweet/tangy, soft/crunchy, warm/fresh.

## Key Technique
**Single layer, high heat, skin-side down.** Crowding the tray makes pumpkin steam instead of caramelize. High heat (220°C) is essential for browning. Skin-side down means the flesh gets maximum contact with the hot tray, creating caramelization while skin crisps up.

## Shortcut
Buy pre-cut pumpkin from supermarket. Use store-bought tzatziki instead of making mint garlic yoghurt. Buy pre-toasted pepitas. Skip pomegranate if not in season. The core — roasted spiced pumpkin on yoghurt — still delivers.

## Variations
- **Vegan**: Use coconut yoghurt or tahini sauce instead of yoghurt
- **Different vegetables**: Works with cauliflower, carrots, sweet potato
- **Nut variations**: Use toasted almonds, pistachios, or pine nuts instead of pepitas
- **Cheese**: Add crumbled feta or goat cheese for richness
- **Grain base**: Serve over couscous or freekeh instead of just yoghurt
- **Spice variations**: Use za'atar, ras el hanout, or harissa paste

## Storage
- **Fridge**: Roasted pumpkin keeps 4 days, store separately from yoghurt. Reheat before assembling.
- **Freezer**: Not ideal - texture suffers
- **Counter**: Assemble and serve immediately

## Make Ahead
Roast pumpkin up to 2 days ahead, reheat before serving. Make yoghurt sauce up to 3 days ahead. Toast pepitas days ahead in airtight container. Assemble just before serving for best presentation.

## Troubleshooting
- **Pumpkin not caramelizing?** Oven not hot enough, or tray crowded. Increase temp, use bigger tray.
- **Pumpkin falling apart?** Overcooked, or wedges cut too thin. Thicker wedges hold shape better.
- **Too sweet?** Add more lemon to yoghurt, or use less honey drizzle
- **Skin tough?** It's edible but some prefer to peel after roasting while warm
- **Bland flavor?** Not enough spice mix, or spices old/stale. Be generous with seasoning.

## Additional Notes
Kent pumpkin (also called Jap pumpkin in Australia) is ideal - dense, sweet, holds shape. Butternut squash is good alternative. Regular orange pumpkin works but can be watery. If pomegranate is expensive or out of season, skip it - the dish still works. The visual appeal of this dish is huge - it looks restaurant-quality which makes it perfect for entertaining. Can be served warm or room temperature, making it good for buffets. Cost is about $8-10 for 4 people, but looks like a $25 restaurant dish. Leftovers reheat well and are great in grain bowls or wraps.
