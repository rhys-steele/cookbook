## Raw Idea
Low and slow — sliced onions cooked in butter and oil for 45+ minutes until deeply golden and sweet. A splash of balsamic at the end optional. Freezes in portions. Unlocks burgers, toasties, tarts, pasta. Shortcut is jarred caramelised onion warmed with a knob of butter.

## Overview
The foundational staple that transforms simple dishes into restaurant-quality meals. Thinly sliced onions slowly cooked in butter and oil until their natural sugars caramelise into deep golden sweetness. Patience is the only real skill required.

## Why This Works
The long, slow cook breaks down the onions' natural sugars through the Maillard reaction, creating hundreds of complex flavor compounds without burning. The combination of butter (for flavor) and oil (for higher smoke point) prevents sticking while adding richness. Salt draws out moisture early, speeding the evaporation process that concentrates the sugars.

## Key Technique
**Patience over heat.** Rushing with high heat gives you burnt onions with a bitter edge, not proper caramelisation. The first 15 minutes build the foundation (soft, translucent), the next 30 create the magic (golden, sweet, jammy). Stir infrequently at first, then more as they darken and start to stick.

## Shortcut
Buy jarred caramelised onions (check ingredients - should just be onions, oil, maybe vinegar). Warm in a pan with a knob of butter to freshen up the flavor. Not quite the same depth, but gets you 90% there in 2 minutes.

## Variations
- **Red wine**: Deglaze with red wine instead of balsamic for deeper, more savory notes
- **Shallots**: Use shallots for sweeter, more delicate flavor (great for French onion soup)
- **Brown sugar**: Add 1 tsp brown sugar in the last 5 minutes for extra caramelisation (not needed but speeds it up)
- **Herbs**: Add fresh thyme or rosemary in the last 10 minutes

## Storage
- **Fridge**: 5 days in an airtight container
- **Freezer**: 3 months in portions - ice cube trays work brilliantly for single-serve portions
- **Counter**: Not recommended

## Make Ahead
Perfect make-ahead staple. Make a big batch on Sunday, portion into ice cube trays, freeze, then pop into bags. Grab cubes as needed for quick weeknight upgrades.

## Troubleshooting
- **Burning on the bottom?** Heat too high. Reduce heat immediately, add splash of water, scrape up stuck bits
- **Not browning after 30 mins?** Too much moisture - be patient, keep cooking. Or your pan might be too small (onions are steaming not frying)
- **Bitter taste?** Burnt. Can't fix - start over. Prevention: lower heat, stir more frequently as they darken
- **Watery and pale?** Heat too low or pan overcrowded. Increase heat slightly, spread onions out

## Uses / Pairs With
The secret weapon that unlocks:
- **Burgers and hot dogs** - Top tier upgrade
- **Grilled cheese / toasties** - With sharp cheddar or gruyere
- **Tarts and pizzas** - Caramelised onion and goat cheese tart is a classic
- **Pasta** - Toss with pasta, goat cheese, walnuts, rocket
- **French onion soup** - The foundation
- **Canapés** - On crostini with blue cheese
- **Steak** - Classic steakhouse move
- **Eggs** - Omelettes, frittatas, quiche

## Additional Notes
The smell will fill your house - open a window. Your eyes won't water much since you're not cutting near the root and the long cook mellows the compounds. Cost: very cheap. Season: year-round. Scales up easily - make 2kg at once if your pan is big enough.
