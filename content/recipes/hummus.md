## Raw Idea
Tinned chickpeas, tahini, lemon, garlic, ice water — blitzed until impossibly smooth. The secrets: more tahini than you think, process the tahini and lemon first to create a creamy base, and blend WAY longer than feels necessary. Serve with olive oil and paprika. Shortcut: buy good hummus, thin with lemon juice, top with excellent olive oil.

## Overview
The ultra-smooth, cloud-like hummus you get at good Middle Eastern restaurants is achievable at home. The key isn't special ingredients — it's technique. Processing tahini and lemon juice first creates an emulsified base that transforms tinned chickpeas into impossibly creamy dip. More blending time, ice water, and good tahini are the non-negotiables.

## Why This Works
Tahini and lemon juice emulsify into a creamy base when processed together first, similar to mayonnaise. This prevents the grainy texture you get when chickpeas are blended alone. Ice water helps the emulsion stay stable while the food processor heats up from friction, and adds lightness. Longer processing time breaks down chickpeas at the cellular level. Good tahini (runny, not thick and bitter) makes all the difference.

## Key Technique
**Process tahini and lemon first.** This creates an emulsified base that chickpeas blend into, rather than trying to blend chickpeas alone (which gives you grainy hummus). It takes 1 minute and changes everything. Then blend the final mixture for 2-3 minutes minimum - longer than you think is necessary.

## Shortcut
Buy good quality hummus from a Middle Eastern shop (avoid supermarket brands with preservatives). Thin it with lemon juice and a splash of ice water, drizzle with your best olive oil, and sprinkle with paprika. Or use canned chickpeas and a good blender - still works, just slightly less smooth than food processor.

## Variations
- **Roasted garlic**: Roast whole garlic bulb until soft, squeeze out cloves, blend in for mellow sweet garlic flavor
- **Spicy**: Add harissa, cayenne, or a fresh chili
- **Roasted red pepper**: Blend in 1 roasted red pepper (jarred is fine)
- **Beetroot**: Add 1 small cooked beetroot for pink hummus
- **Herb**: Add bunch of fresh coriander or parsley for green hummus
- **Extra creamy**: Peel chickpeas (tedious but gives ultra-smooth result)
- **Traditional**: Top with whole chickpeas, pine nuts, and paprika

## Storage
- **Fridge**: 5 days in airtight container, bring to room temp before serving
- **Freezer**: 3 months - texture slightly changes but still good
- **Counter**: Not recommended

## Make Ahead
Make 2-3 days ahead - flavors actually improve. Store with plastic wrap pressed directly onto surface to prevent skin forming. Bring to room temperature 30 minutes before serving and re-drizzle with fresh olive oil.

## Troubleshooting
- **Grainy texture?** Process longer (3-5 minutes total), or you didn't process tahini and lemon first, or your tahini is old/poor quality
- **Too thick?** Add more ice water or reserved chickpea liquid, 1 tablespoon at a time
- **Tastes bitter?** Cheap tahini or too much garlic. Use less garlic or roast it first. Buy better tahini.
- **Not creamy enough?** Need more tahini, or blend longer, or add more ice water
- **Too garlicky?** Use 1 clove instead of 2, or use roasted garlic instead of raw
- **Doesn't taste like restaurant hummus?** Needs more tahini, more lemon, and longer blending time

## Uses / Pairs With
Endless applications:
- **Mezze platter** - With vegetables, pita, olives, feta
- **Sandwich spread** - Instead of mayo on any sandwich
- **Bowl base** - Under grains, roasted vegetables, proteins
- **Dip** - Raw vegetables, crackers, chips
- **Sauce** - Thin with water and use as sauce for falafel, shawarma
- **Toast** - Breakfast toast with olive oil and za'atar
- **Pasta** - Thin with pasta water as a quick sauce

## Additional Notes
Tahini quality matters enormously - look for runny, pourable tahini that's light in color. Avoid thick, paste-like tahini that separates with oil on top and bitter paste at bottom. Middle Eastern shops stock better tahini than supermarkets. The ice water trick is crucial - room temperature or warm water won't create the same silky texture. Some people peel chickpeas for ultra-smooth hummus but it's tedious and not necessary if you blend long enough. Fresh lemon juice (not bottled) makes a noticeable difference. This recipe scales easily - double or triple it if you're meal prepping. Costs about $2 to make and beats store-bought hollow hummus.
