## Raw Idea
Softened butter mixed with fresh parsley, chives, garlic, and lemon zest. Roll into a log, freeze, slice off rounds as needed. Melts over steaks, fish, vegetables, or toast and transforms them into something special. Store-bought garlic butter activated with fresh herbs and lemon is the shortcut.

## Overview
The French bistro secret weapon. Herb butter (beurre composé) is softened butter mixed with aromatics, rolled into a log, and kept in the freezer ready to deploy. A single round melting over a hot steak, piece of fish, or roasted vegetable adds instant richness, brightness, and restaurant polish.

## Why This Works
Butter is pure fat, which means it's a perfect vehicle for fat-soluble flavor compounds from herbs and garlic. When the cold butter round hits hot food, it melts slowly and bastes the food, creating a glossy sauce. The herbs stay vibrant. The garlic mellows. The lemon zest adds brightness that cuts through richness. It's basically a built-in sauce that requires zero technique.

## Key Technique
**Mix until evenly distributed.** You want every slice to have herbs, garlic, and lemon throughout. No streaks of plain butter. Beat it thoroughly until it lightens in color slightly - this incorporates air and makes it more spreadable.

## Shortcut
Buy store-bought garlic butter and stir in fresh chopped herbs and lemon zest. Or just use plain butter and sprinkle fresh herbs and garlic on your food. It won't be quite as elegant but the flavors are there.

## Variations
- **Steak butter**: Add minced shallots and a splash of red wine
- **Seafood butter**: Add tarragon and white wine instead of chives
- **Spicy**: Add red chili flakes or finely chopped fresh chili
- **Anchovy butter**: Add 2-3 mashed anchovy fillets for umami bomb
- **Blue cheese butter**: Mix in crumbled blue cheese (amazing on steak)
- **Garlic-only**: Skip herbs, double the garlic, add parsley at the end

## Storage
- **Fridge**: 1 week wrapped tightly
- **Freezer**: 3 months - slice off rounds as needed straight from freezer
- **Counter**: Not recommended unless using same day

## Make Ahead
This IS a make-ahead staple. Make a log on Sunday, slice throughout the week. Or make several logs at once and freeze. Having herb butter in the freezer makes you feel like you have your life together.

## Troubleshooting
- **Butter won't soften?** Microwave in 5-second bursts, but don't let it melt
- **Herbs turning brown?** They weren't dry enough when mixed in. Pat them dry after chopping.
- **Butter too soft to roll?** Chill it for 20 minutes first, then shape
- **Tastes too garlicky?** You can use less, or blanch the garlic in boiling water for 30 seconds to mellow it
- **Won't slice cleanly?** Let the log warm up for 5 minutes before slicing, or use a hot knife

## Uses / Pairs With
Transforms everything it touches:
- **Steak** - The classic. Let a round melt on top of a resting steak
- **Grilled fish** - Especially good on white fish, salmon, or prawns
- **Vegetables** - Toss with roasted vegetables or steamed greens
- **Pasta** - Toss with hot pasta and pasta water for instant sauce
- **Bread** - Garlic bread upgrade, or on dinner rolls
- **Chicken** - Under the skin before roasting, or on grilled chicken
- **Potatoes** - On baked potatoes, mashed into mash, or on fries
- **Eggs** - Scrambled eggs become luxurious
- **Corn** - On grilled or boiled corn on the cob

## Additional Notes
Use European-style butter if you can - it has higher fat content and tastes richer. The butter MUST be softened, not melted - melted butter won't hold the shape or texture. You can use salted butter if that's what you have, just skip the added salt. This is an endlessly customizable template - once you master the basic technique, you can create any flavor profile you want. It's also a great way to use up herbs before they go bad. Make a double batch - you'll use it faster than you think.
