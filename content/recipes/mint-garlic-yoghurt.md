## Raw Idea
Greek yoghurt blitzed with fresh mint, garlic, lemon, and olive oil. Five minutes to make, transforms anything it touches. The cooling counterpoint to spicy food, the bright lift for rich dishes. Use it as sauce for lamb, chicken, falafel, or as the creamy base for Greek salad.

## Overview
The essential Middle Eastern condiment. Fresh mint and sharp garlic tempered by creamy yoghurt and brightened with lemon. It's cooling, refreshing, and versatile — a sauce that works as well dolloped on spicy chicken as it does stirred into salads or drizzled over roasted vegetables.

## Why This Works
Yoghurt's lactic acid and cream provide a cooling, rich base that mellows raw garlic's harshness. Fresh mint adds aromatic brightness that complements the tang. Lemon juice reinforces the acidity while adding complexity. Olive oil (optional but recommended) adds richness and helps the sauce cling to food. The combination creates a balanced sauce that's simultaneously cooling, bright, rich, and refreshing.

## Key Technique
**Grate the garlic to paste.** Finely grated or pasted garlic distributes evenly throughout the yoghurt and mellows faster than minced chunks. Use a microplane or the fine side of a box grater. If you mince it, you'll get harsh garlic bombs in some bites.

## Shortcut
Buy tzatziki and stir in fresh chopped mint. Or use store-bought plain yoghurt and just add lemon juice, garlic powder, and dried mint (not as good but works in a pinch). The fresh mint really makes this special though.

## Variations
- **Cucumber mint yoghurt (tzatziki-style)**: Add grated cucumber (squeezed dry) and dill
- **Spicy**: Add pinch of cayenne or chili flakes
- **Herby**: Add fresh dill or parsley along with mint
- **Middle Eastern**: Add ground cumin and coriander
- **Thinner sauce**: Add 1-2 tbsp water or olive oil to make it more drizzle-able
- **Tahini yoghurt**: Replace half the yoghurt with tahini for nutty richness

## Storage
- **Fridge**: 3-4 days in airtight container (flavors intensify over time)
- **Freezer**: Not recommended - yoghurt separates when frozen
- **Counter**: Not recommended - needs refrigeration

## Make Ahead
Make up to 3 days ahead. Actually improves after sitting in fridge for a few hours as flavors meld. Stir before using as it may separate slightly.

## Troubleshooting
- **Too garlicky?** Use less garlic next time, or add more yoghurt to dilute. You can also add a pinch of sugar to balance.
- **Too tangy?** Add a pinch of sugar or more olive oil to round it out
- **Too thick?** Thin with water, lemon juice, or olive oil until desired consistency
- **Mint turned brown?** Add mint closer to serving time, or use very sharp knife to avoid bruising
- **Separated or watery?** Give it a good stir - yoghurt naturally separates. If using low-fat yoghurt, it separates more.

## Uses / Pairs With
The universal upgrade:
- **Greek salad** - Use as the creamy base for feta-melt dressing
- **Lamb** - Classic pairing with roasted or grilled lamb
- **Chicken** - Marinade base or serving sauce for grilled chicken
- **Falafel** - Essential accompaniment
- **Roasted vegetables** - Especially good with eggplant, zucchini, pumpkin
- **Grain bowls** - Drizzle over rice, quinoa, or couscous bowls
- **Kebabs** - Any grilled meat skewers
- **Pita and flatbreads** - Dipping sauce
- **Eggs** - Poached eggs, shakshuka
- **Spicy food** - Cooling element for curries, tacos, hot wings

## Additional Notes
Full-fat Greek yoghurt makes a huge difference here - low-fat versions are watery and lack richness. If you can't find Greek yoghurt, strain regular yoghurt through cheesecloth for an hour to thicken it. Fresh mint is non-negotiable - dried mint is a completely different flavor and won't give you that bright, cooling quality. This sauce is so versatile and quick that it should be in regular rotation. Make a batch on Sunday and use it all week on different dishes. Cost is minimal (under $3) and it makes everything taste fresh and intentional.
