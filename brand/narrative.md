# Brand & Narrative Quick Reference

One-page reference for the Snap to Plan voice, story, and system.

---

## The Story

A software engineer who cooks constantly noticed the patterns: a handful of staples plus repeatable methods build into great meals. The hardest part is always planning.

So they built a cookbook designed like a product — clean typography, grids, reusable components, predictable templates — and a companion web experience that turns any recipe into a menu and shopping list in seconds.

**This is software you can eat.**

---

## The Hook: Snap to Plan

A recurring motif with dual meaning:

1. **Snapping fingers** — effortless planning (cover concept)
2. **Snapping a QR** — scan to plan instantly (Snap Codes)

### Rules

- Subtle and structural, never loud or gimmicky
- Never overlays hero photography
- Appears once per recipe page as a footer/meta strip
- QR codes are called "Snap Codes"
- Consistent placement, minimal copy

## Voice

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Confident, minimal | Flowery, aspirational |
| Warm but direct | Cold or corporate |
| Product-driven | Marketing-speak |
| Clear definitions | Vague promises |
| Founder-y | Agency-y |

---

## Language Rules

### Do

- State facts: "Serves 4. 30 minutes."
- Give clear actions: "Build a menu around this"
- Be specific: "Caramelised onions unlock burgers, toasties, and tarts"
- Use the system language: staples, dishes, menus, plans

### Don't

- "Discover your culinary journey"
- "Unlock the magic of home cooking"
- "Transform your kitchen experience"
- "Elevate your meals"
- Any superlative without substance
- "Passionate about food"
- "Curated with love"

## Design Principles (for copy)

1. **Structural consistency** — same patterns everywhere
2. **Reduce cognitive load** — defaults over options
3. **Fast** — scan → plan → list in under 60 seconds
4. **Quiet confidence** — let the system speak

## Quick Checks

Before any copy ships, ask:

- [ ] Would a founder write this?
- [ ] Is it specific or vague?
- [ ] Does it sound like a product or a lifestyle brand?
- [ ] Is it necessary?
